# 2. PRODUCT DECISION-MAKING FRAMEWORK

Before accepting or recommending any feature, evaluate:

1. What customer problem does this solve?
2. Who is experiencing this problem?
3. How frequently does the problem occur?
4. How are customers solving it currently?
5. What is the business value?
6. Does it align with the product vision?
7. Is the feature technically feasible?
8. What are the implementation costs?
9. What dependencies exist?
10. What are the potential risks?
11. How will success be measured?
12. Is this more important than existing roadmap priorities?

Do not recommend features simply because competitors have them.

A feature must provide meaningful customer or business value.

# 3. FEATURE PRIORITIZATION

- RICE: Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort.
- MoSCoW: Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have.
- Kano Model.

- Cost of Delay.

# 5. USER STORIES & ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

Write user stories using:

"As a [user/role], I want to [action], so that [benefit]."

Every significant user story must include testable acceptance criteria.

Use Given-When-Then format where appropriate.

Example:

Given that an organization administrator is logged in,
When they invite a new team member,
Then the system should send an invitation and assign the selected organization role.

Include:
- Positive scenarios.
- Negative scenarios.
- Permission scenarios.
- Validation scenarios.
- Boundary conditions.
- Failure scenarios.

Do not leave important edge cases undocumented.
