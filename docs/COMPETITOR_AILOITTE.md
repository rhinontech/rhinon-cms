## 3. The content plays they run

1. **Glossary hub (`/topics/`).** These are "What is X" pages on high-volume AI terms (what is AI, conversational AI, speech AI, data warehousing, sentiment analysis, AI ethics). They rarely reach the top 10, but the volumes are so large that position 60–85 still brings visits. They also build topical relevance for "AI development".
2. **"What is" and "X vs Y" explainers.** Examples: MVP, PoC vs prototype vs MVP, wireframe vs prototype vs mockup, TypeScript vs JavaScript, Android vs iOS, React vs Angular, onshore vs offshore vs nearshore. Each wins a cluster of long-tail queries and featured snippets.
3. **"How to build an app like X" and cost guides.** DoorDash, OctaFX, Netflix, food delivery, dating, fitness, e-learning, stock trading, marketplace. These pull in founders at the idea stage.
4. **Self-published listicles.** Examples: "Top software development companies in India", "Android app development companies in Australia", "Top app developers in Bangalore", "Healthcare software companies in Germany". These target commercial "companies in X" searches and get cited in AI Overviews.
5. **Vertical depth in healthcare.** HIPAA, FHIR R4, HL7, EMR vs EHR, revenue cycle management, AI in healthcare ethics, plus dedicated landing pages (`/hipaa-compliant-software-development/`, `/emr-and-ehr-software-development/`). Owning one vertical deeply builds authority in it.
6. **Case studies named after clients.** BankSathi, iPatientCare, SupDup and MedCompass pages rank for the clients' brand names, so people searching for the client find Ailoitte.
7. **Programmatic "hire" pages.** `/hire-ios-developers/`, `/hire-shopify-developers/`, and so on.

## 4. How they built their links

Sampled from the strongest of their 1,032 referring domains with a spam score under 30:

| Link type | Examples | Rhinon Labs equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Ranking and listicle sites | mobileappdaily.com (199 links), topdevelopers.co, findbestfirms.com, primefirms.co, businessfirms.co, c2creview.co | C-05: pitch listicles; Clutch, GoodFirms, DesignRush |
| Developer communities | dev.to (44 links), hackernoon.com (1 sponsored) | Cross-post founder articles on dev.to and Hashnode |
| Press release, news and guest-post networks | timebusinessnews.com, entrepreneursbreak.com, maplegrovelocalnews.com, storeboard.com, apsense.com, iotglobalnetwork.com | Selective. Many of these are low quality (see below) |
| Social bookmarking and article directories | interestpin.com, viesearch.com, sooperarticle.com, uploadyourblogs.com, articlepluspost.com, rumorcircle.com, palscity.com | **Do not copy** |

- Platform split of all their links: 2,851 from organisation sites, 2,428 blogs, 875 news.
- About 39% are nofollow.
- Their overall spam score is a moderate 15.

**Don't copy:** a large share of their links are bookmarking, article-directory and paid guest-post links. Those are past-era SEO tactics, and Google largely discounts them. Copy the listicle, directory, developer-community and genuine media parts only. Rhinon Labs' current 31 linking domains are already all spam (audit C-05), so adding more low-quality links would make that worse.

## 5. What Rhinon Labs should take from this

Ranked by impact for Rhinon Labs' positioning: AI automation agency, MVPs for founders, and India plus US.

| # | Play | Ailoitte proof | Rhinon Labs action | Ties to audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Listicle and directory presence | Most of their high-rank links | Clutch, GoodFirms and DesignRush profiles; pitch mobileappdaily, topdevelopers, hexagondigital, torpedoweb, morismedia | C-04, C-05 |
| 2 | One definitive "What is an MVP" page | Their #2 traffic page; ranks for 6+ MVP variants | Write the best "What is an MVP (with real costs and timelines)" page and link it to /services/mvp-development | H-05 MVP cluster |
| 3 | "X vs Y" explainers in your clusters | PoC vs prototype vs MVP, onshore vs offshore | "AI agent vs chatbot vs automation", "n8n vs Zapier vs custom", "MVP vs prototype vs PoC", "In-house vs agency vs freelancer" | H-05, M-07 |
| 4 | Pick one vertical and go deep | Healthcare: HIPAA, FHIR, EMR | Construction and fit-out (Apexis Pro, SR Fitout) is your existing proof, so own "construction snagging software", "fit-out project management software" and similar | H-03 step 6 |
| 5 | Case studies titled with client names | BankSathi ranks for "banksathi" | Title case studies "Apexis Pro", "SR Fitout", "RichHarbor", "Al Rooha" with the outcome | M-06 |
| 6 | Developer-community syndication | dev.to (44 links) | Cross-post technical build write-ups (RAG chatbot, AI lead research) to dev.to and Hashnode with a canonical link | C-05, H-10 |
| 7 | "How to build an app like X" and cost guides | DoorDash, OctaFX, fitness, dating | Only where it matches your services. Your current cost guides already cover this; fix the blank ones first | C-02, H-07 |
| 8 | Self-published "Top companies" listicles | 3 of their 8 AI Overview citations | Use with care. Only publish if it's genuinely useful and transparent about including yourself; otherwise it reads as a trust risk (C-06) | — |

**Skip:**
- High-volume glossary terms ("what is AI", 165k/mo). They bring traffic at position 60+ but almost no leads, and your domain has no authority yet.
- Bookmarking and article-directory links.
- Spreading content across every app type. Ailoitte has 1,400+ referring domains to carry that breadth; Rhinon Labs should stay inside its four clusters (audit M-07).

## 6. How to repeat this for any competitor

For each competitor domain:

1. **Domain overview** (`domain_rank_overview`, India and US): ranked keyword count, top-10 count, estimated traffic. This shows whether they really rank or only look big.
2. **Ranked keywords** (`ranked_keywords`, sorted by estimated traffic, then filtered to top 30): the exact keywords and the page that ranks for each.
3. **Top pages** (`relevant_pages`): which pages bring the traffic, which shows their content strategy.
4. **AI Overview citations** (`ranked_keywords` with `item_types: ai_overview_reference`): where AI search quotes them.
5. **Backlinks** (`backlinks_summary`, then `referring_domains` filtered to spam score under 30): how they built authority.
6. **Paid keywords** (`ranked_keywords` with `item_types: paid`): what they pay for. Ailoitte runs no Google Ads in this data.
7. **Gap list** (`domain_intersection` with `intersections: false`): keywords they rank for and you don't.
